Word Roots
Latin Root: fort — "strong"

The Latin root fort comes from fortis, the Latin word for "strong." St. Augustine lasted partly because it was strong: first wooden forts, then the stone Castillo de San Marcos.

📖

🔎 Discovery Fact
The Castillo de San Marcos has never been captured in battle. It did change owners: Spain, then Great Britain, then Spain again, then the United States. But each time, the change came from a treaty (an agreement between countries), not from an attack.

Learn the Story
📖
The Founding of St. Augustine, 1565
Five events. One settlement. A chain of causes and effects.

By the mid-1500s, Spain had been visiting Florida's coasts for over fifty years, but no Spanish settlement (a place where people come to live) had lasted. That changed in 1565. Read this short timeline carefully. Notice how each event sets up the next.

Event 1 · 1564: The French arrive first. French settlers build Fort Caroline near the mouth of the St. Johns River (today, Jacksonville). Most of them are Huguenots, French Protestants. Protestants are Christians who broke away from the Catholic Church. Spain sees the fort as a threat, a danger, because Spain claims all of Florida. A claim is when a country says, “This land is ours.”

Event 2 · Spring 1565: Spain responds. King Philip II of Spain orders Pedro Menéndez de Avilés to remove the French and start a Spanish settlement on Florida's east coast. That summer, Menéndez sails from Spain with his ships.

Event 3 · September 8, 1565: St. Augustine is founded. Menéndez lands on the Florida coast, claims the harbor for Spain, and names the settlement San Agustín (St. Augustine). He picked the name because he first saw this coast on August 28, the feast day of Saint Augustine. A feast day is a day the Catholic Church sets aside to remember a saint. The Timucua people already live in the area.

Event 4 · Late September 1565: Spanish forces capture Fort Caroline. Menéndez marches north and takes the French fort. Without Fort Caroline, France no longer has a place to stand in Florida. No other European country has a settlement there now, so Spain's claim is much stronger.

Event 5 · The years that follow. St. Augustine survives hurricanes, attacks, and food shortages. In 1672, workers begin building the Castillo de San Marcos, a stone fort that still stands today. The workers included Native people, enslaved Africans, and Spanish soldiers. The fort is made of coquina, a stone made of tiny shells. Much later, in 1738, the free Black community of Fort Mose forms just north of St. Augustine. People who escaped slavery in the English colonies found freedom there.

Why this matters. St. Augustine is the oldest European-founded city in the 50 states where people have lived every year since it began. It is older than Jamestown (1607) and Plymouth (1620). Florida's European story started in Spanish — not English.

Connect the Ideas
💡 Key Concept

St. Augustine, founded by Pedro Menéndez de Avilés in 1565, is the oldest European-founded city in the 50 states where people have lived every year since it began. It exists because of a chain of events: the French built a fort on land Spain claimed, Spain sent Menéndez to push them out, and he built the town on Timucua land.

🤠 Meanwhile in Texas…

St. Augustine (1565) is the oldest permanent European settlement in what became the United States — and it was founded because Spain feared French competition. Texas has a parallel story, just 120 years later. When La Salle planted a French flag on Matagorda Bay in 1685, Spain reacted the same way it had to Fort Caroline: with alarm and action. Spain launched multiple expeditions to find and destroy La Salle's colony, and then built missions and presidios across East Texas to block any future French claims. The most important cluster became the five San Antonio missions along the San Antonio River — including Mission San Antonio de Valero, which you know today as the Alamo.

The lesson is the same in both states: Spain didn't build settlements just to explore — it built them to hold territory against rivals. Pedro Menéndez founded St. Augustine to answer France's Fort Caroline. Spanish missionaries built San Antonio to answer France's Fort Saint Louis. And both settlements survived — St. Augustine behind its coquina walls, San Antonio around its river and springs — because they were placed where geography offered natural advantages for defense, water, and farming.

🦉
🌴 Opti says…
Lab archive note: the word coquina comes from Spanish for "little shells." The walls of the Castillo de San Marcos are made of shell stone dug from quarries on nearby Anastasia Island. When cannonballs hit coquina, the soft stone absorbs the blow instead of shattering. Florida's geology helped St. Augustine survive — places shape history.
