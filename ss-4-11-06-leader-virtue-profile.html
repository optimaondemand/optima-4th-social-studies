Character in Action
Building a virtue profile of a Florida leader

A virtue profile is more than a biography (the story of a person's life). A virtue is a strong, good character trait, like courage or service. A virtue profile goes beyond what someone did and asks which virtue their actions showed. When we say a leader showed courage, we mean they took a risk or stood up for something when it was hard. When we say they showed service, we mean they put others before themselves. When we say they showed perseverance, we mean they kept going even when things went wrong.

Let's look at Mary McLeod Bethune as a model. She was born in 1875 in South Carolina, the child of parents who had been enslaved. She believed that education could change lives, and she didn't just believe it, she acted on it. In 1904, Daytona had no good school for Black children. So in October 1904 she rented a small house and started the Daytona Educational and Industrial Training School for Negro Girls with five girls, her own young son, and $1.50. To raise money, she sold sweet potato pies. That school grew into Bethune-Cookman University.

What virtues did Bethune show? Courage: she opened a school with almost no money in a town where Black children had nowhere good to learn. Service: she spent her life working for her students, not to get rich. Perseverance: she faced racism, poverty, and doubt, and she kept going. Every one of these claims has evidence, a real fact that proves it: the five girls and $1.50, the pies, the school that is still open today.

To build your own virtue profile, pick a Florida leader you've studied in this unit. Think about what they did and which virtue their actions showed. Then find the evidence: a specific event, a decision, a quote, or a result that proves the virtue was real. The virtues you can choose from include courage, service, honesty, courtesy, perseverance, and self-governance (making good choices and following rules without being told). The strongest profiles connect actions to virtues with clear, specific details.

Leaders were not perfect. They had money troubles, made hard choices, and sometimes lost. Bethune had to sell pies to keep her school going. A virtue profile does not say a leader was perfect. It shows real evidence of one strong trait. This lesson pulls together everything you learned in Unit 11. Civil rights leaders, community builders, immigrants, citizens who spoke up, volunteers who served: they all showed virtues through their actions.

Connect the Ideas
💡 Key Concept

A virtue profile helps students explain how character and action can serve Florida communities — connecting what leaders did to why it mattered.

🤠 Meanwhile in Texas…

Texas has its own gallery of leaders whose virtues shaped the state. Sam Houston showed courage by opposing secession when nearly every other Texas politician supported it — he lost the governorship rather than abandon his principles. Barbara Jordan showed integrity during the Watergate hearings, reminding the nation that the Constitution belongs to all Americans. And Bessie Coleman showed perseverance by traveling across an ocean to earn a pilot's license that no American school would give her. Each leader's actions reveal a specific virtue — and that virtue is what made their contribution lasting.

Building a virtue profile works the same way whether you're studying a Florida leader or a Texas one: you start with what the person did, then ask what character trait made that action meaningful. Bethune's school wasn't just a building — it was an act of courage and service. Houston's stand against secession wasn't just a political move — it was an act of integrity. The evidence doesn't change, but the virtue you identify depends on which part of the leader's story you focus on. That's what makes this kind of historical thinking both challenging and important.

Word Roots
Latin Root: dign — "worthy"

The Latin word dignus means "worthy," or deserving of respect. The root dign is in words about the worth every person has, and about how people should be treated.

📖

dignity · the worth every person has, which means they deserve respect · dignified · calm, serious, and worthy of respect · dignitary · an important person, like a leader or official, at a special event · indignant · angry because something is unfair

🔎 Discovery Fact
In 2010 the Florida Legislature created the Florida Civil Rights Hall of Fame in the Capitol in Tallahassee, to honor people who worked for the dignity and equal rights of all Floridians. Its first members, chosen in 2012, were Mary McLeod Bethune, Rev. C. K. Steele, and Claude Pepper. A place of honor shows that people are worthy, and that is what dign means.
