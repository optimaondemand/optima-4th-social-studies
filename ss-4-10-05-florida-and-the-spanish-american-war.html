Florida and the Spanish-American War
How Florida's location made it the starting place for a war in 1898

Why was there a war? In 1898, the United States went to war with Spain. Back then, Spain ruled Cuba, an island just 90 miles south of Key West. Many Cubans were fighting for independence (the freedom to rule themselves), and many Americans wanted Spain to leave Cuba. In February 1898, the U.S. battleship (a big warship) Maine blew up in Havana Harbor in Cuba. Many Americans blamed Spain, but no one has ever proved what caused the explosion. Many experts today think it was an accident inside the ship. About two months later, the war began.

Why Florida? Look at a map. Cuba is closer to Florida than to any other part of the United States. That made Florida the best place to gather troops (groups of soldiers), load supplies and send ships to Cuba. The Army chose Tampa as its main staging ground, the place where soldiers, horses, weapons and supplies were gathered and organized before they shipped out. Tampa had what the Army needed: a port, and a railroad that ran right out to the ships at Port Tampa. Key West was even closer to Cuba, but in 1898 no railroad reached it yet.

Tampa fills up. In the spring of 1898, about 30,000 soldiers came to Tampa. That was a lot of people for a small city. In 1890, only 5,532 people lived in Tampa! Soldiers camped in tents in the sandy fields, practiced marching in the heat, and waited for orders. Army leaders and newspaper reporters stayed at Henry Plant’s fancy Tampa Bay Hotel. Among the soldiers were the famous Rough Riders, a cavalry regiment (a group of about 1,000 soldiers who fight on horseback). They were volunteers, people who signed up on their own. Colonel Leonard Wood led them at first. Theodore Roosevelt, who later became president, was second in command and later took over.

The jam at Port Tampa. Tampa was not ready for so many people. Only one railroad track ran the nine miles from Tampa to Port Tampa, where the ships waited at a long pier (a dock that sticks out into the water). Trains full of soldiers, horses, food and weapons lined up on the tracks, and soldiers sometimes waited all night for a train. Theodore Roosevelt wrote that Tampa sat “at the end of a one-track railroad” and that everything was in a tangle. When it was time to sail, there was no room on the ships for most of the horses, so the Rough Riders left their horses behind in Tampa. In June 1898, the ships finally steamed out of Tampa Bay toward Cuba.

The fighting was far away. Florida was the launchpad, the place the war was launched from. The battles happened in Cuba, in Puerto Rico and in the Philippines (islands in Asia that Spain also ruled). In Florida, Key West was an important Navy base, a home port for warships, close to Cuba. In Jacksonville, about 30,000 soldiers gathered at Camp Cuba Libre (“Free Cuba”). The fighting ended before most of them ever reached Cuba.

After the war. The fighting stopped in August 1898, after about four months. In December, Spain and the United States signed a treaty (a written agreement that ends a war). Spain gave up Cuba, which became its own country in 1902, and Spain gave Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines to the United States. The war helped change Tampa from a small town into a city: 5,532 people lived there in 1890, and 15,839 in 1900. Newspapers all over the country had written about Florida. In Ybor City, many of the Cuban cigar workers you met last lesson had hoped for this day for years. They had given money to help Cuba win its freedom, and in 1891 the Cuban leader José Martí came to Tampa to ask for their help.

Connect the Ideas
💡 Key Concept

Florida's location, close to Cuba with a long coastline, made it the launchpad for the Spanish-American War in 1898. Tampa was the main staging ground, Key West was a Navy base, and Jacksonville had Camp Cuba Libre. The war brought national attention to Florida and helped Tampa grow into a city.

🤠 Meanwhile in Texas…

Tampa was Florida's staging ground for the Spanish-American War — and Texas played a role too. San Antonio was where Theodore Roosevelt recruited and trained his famous Rough Riders at Camp Wood (later Fort Sam Houston) before they shipped to Tampa and then Cuba. Texas volunteers made up a large portion of the U.S. forces. The war also mattered to Texas because of its shared border with Mexico and deep cultural connections to the Spanish-speaking world — the same connections that made Florida's proximity to Cuba so strategically important. After the war, both states saw military bases expand permanently, shaping their economies for decades to come.

Here's the geographic lesson both states illustrate: location determines military importance. Florida was chosen because it was the closest point to Cuba. San Antonio was chosen because it already had military infrastructure and was close to the Mexican border. In wartime, geography isn't just about maps — it's about strategy. The same coastlines and borders that shaped trade and settlement also shaped where armies trained, where ships launched, and which communities grew because of military spending.

Word Roots
Latin Root: mar — "sea"

The Latin word mare means "sea." You can find it in many English words as mar. In 1898, the sea was the only way to get an army from Florida to Cuba, so these sea words were everywhere in Tampa and Key West.

🔎 Discovery Fact
U.S. Marines are soldiers who go to war by sea. In June 1898, a group of Marines sailed from Key West to Guantánamo Bay in Cuba. They were the first American unit to raise the U.S. flag on Cuban land.
