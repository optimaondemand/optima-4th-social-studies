Matching Problems to the Right Level
Local vs. state government responsibilities in Florida

Florida has one state government. It also has 67 counties and more than 400 cities, towns, and villages, and each one has a government of its own. So when something goes wrong, who fixes it? Not every problem goes to the same level of government. Some problems belong to your city or county. That is the local level, the government closest to home. Other problems belong to the state. Each level has its own responsibilities, the jobs it is in charge of. When citizens know the difference, problems get solved faster, because they are talking to the right people.

City government. A city or town is led by a mayor and a city council (some cities call it a city commission), a small group that voters elect to make the city's rules. Cities take care of things close to your front door: city streets and sidewalks, streetlights, city parks, police and fire departments, garbage pickup, and clean water. Cities also decide zoning, the rules about what can be built where. If the playground at your neighborhood park needs new equipment, that is a local problem.

County government. Every city sits inside a county, and a county covers a bigger area. A county is run by a county commission, a group that voters elect. Counties take care of county roads, public libraries, county parks, and the sheriff's office, and they keep the records of who owns each piece of land. Almost half of all Floridians do not live inside any city at all. For them, the county is the local government.

State government. The state of Florida handles bigger problems, the ones that touch people all across the state. Voters elect the governor, who leads the state, and the state senators and state representatives who make its laws in Tallahassee. Together, those lawmakers are called the Legislature. The state builds and fixes state highways and bridges. It sets the standards, the list of what students should learn in each grade. The standards are statewide, which means they are the same all across Florida. It runs state parks and protects Florida's land, water, and wildlife in places like the Everglades. It gives licenses (official permission to do a job) to people like doctors and teachers, after they show they are ready. It also decides how to spend the state's money. If Florida changes what every fourth grader should learn in math, that is a state decision.

How citizens take part. Citizens can participate, or take part, in solving problems. When you see a problem, the first question is, "Who handles this?" Think about how many people the problem touches. If a streetlight on your street is broken, call city hall. If you have a question about state tests, contact your state representative or the Florida Department of Education. Citizens can speak at a city council meeting or a county commission meeting, write to the lawmakers who represent them in Tallahassee, or sign a petition, a letter that many people sign to ask for a change. The key is to bring the problem to the right level.

Why it matters. When citizens know how government works, they can take part responsibly. Instead of just complaining, they can ask the right people for help. Local and state governments solve different kinds of problems. Citizens who understand the difference can advocate, or speak up, for their communities and be heard.

Connect the Ideas
💡 Key Concept

Local and state governments solve different kinds of problems, and citizens can help by participating responsibly and bringing issues to the right level.

🤠 Meanwhile in Texas…

Texas faces many of the same local-vs.-state government questions as Florida, but with its own twist. With 254 counties — more than any other state — Texas has a massive network of local governments handling roads, law enforcement, and property records. Cities like Houston, Dallas, and San Antonio have their own mayors and city councils making decisions about zoning, public safety, and city services. Meanwhile, the state government in Austin handles issues that cross county lines: water rights across drought-prone regions, highway systems connecting distant cities, and statewide school funding formulas.

The pattern is the same in both states: local problems need local solutions, and statewide problems need state-level coordination. A pothole in a Tampa neighborhood gets fixed by the city, just like one in Houston does. But managing water during a drought across West Texas requires the state — just like managing hurricane response across Florida's coast does. Citizens in both states have to know which door to knock on, and that knowledge is what turns a frustrated person into an effective citizen. The skill of knowing your levels of government works the same everywhere.

Word Roots
Latin Root: solv / solut — "loosen"

The Latin word solvere means "to loosen" or "to untie." The roots solv and solut are in many words about working something loose. To solve a problem is to work it loose, like a stuck knot.

📖

solve · to find the answer to a problem · solution · the answer to a problem · resolve · to end a problem or a disagreement for good · dissolve · to mix into a liquid and seem to disappear, the way sugar does in water

🔎 Discovery Fact
Florida sits on a rock called limestone, and rainwater slowly dissolves it, loosening the rock a tiny bit at a time. Over thousands of years, that is how Florida got its caves, its springs, and its sinkholes. Solving a problem works the same way: you loosen it bit by bit until it comes free.
