This Week at a Glance

Last week your student studied a war and a land boom that went bust. This week they follow Florida through hard years and into a new kind of economy (the way people in a place earn and spend money). In Lesson 7 they learn how the Great Depression hurt Florida, how New Deal programs gave people work, and how World War II filled Florida with training bases and shipyards. In Lesson 8 they see how tourism and the space program brought visitors and jobs after the war. The big idea: hard times and new industries can change a place, and one cause can have many effects. This week closes Unit 10.

History skill
Cause and effect. Your student traces what happened because of the Depression, the war, Walt Disney World and the space program, and backs it up with real photos.
Virtue this week
Courage. Families who kept going through hard times, and people who took big risks, like building a giant park on swampy land or sending astronauts to the moon.

Good to know: Lesson 7 tells how, on April 10, 1942, a German submarine sank the oil tanker Gulfamerica close enough to see from Jacksonville Beach, and your student hears two lines from the U.S. Navy’s own war diary for that night. Lesson 8 includes a real NASA recording of the Apollo 11 countdown.

4.10.07Lesson 7 Florida During the Great Depression and World War II
In 1929 the stock market (where people buy and sell small pieces of companies) crashed, and the Great Depression began. Florida was already hurting from the land bust and the 1928 hurricane around Lake Okeechobee; now tourists stopped coming and many people lost their jobs, savings and homes. President Franklin Roosevelt’s New Deal gave people work: the CCC (Civilian Conservation Corps) built state parks, planted trees and fixed roads, and the WPA built schools and bridges. In 1941 the United States entered World War II. Florida’s warm weather and flat land were good for training all year, so the military built bases like NAS Jacksonville, Eglin Field and MacDill Field. In 1942 enemy submarines sank ships near the coast, and towns dimmed their lights at night. Shipyards in Jacksonville, Tampa and Panama City built ships, women took factory jobs, and families used ration books. After the war, many soldiers who had trained in Florida came back to live.
Source: CCC workers building a dining hall at the camp that became O’Leno State Park, July 1935 (State Archives of Florida)Virtue: CourageWord root: serv = to serve or keep safe		 Historian’s Notebook entry (3–5 sentences): one way the Depression hurt Florida and one New Deal or wartime change that helped, plus a Historian’s Sentence.
4.10.08Lesson 8 Tourism, the Space Program, and Florida’s Economy
Tourists came to Florida long before Walt Disney World, but after World War II tourism grew faster than ever: soldiers came back with their families, new highways like Interstate 4 (finished in 1965) made driving easier, flights got cheaper, and air conditioning made summers comfortable. On November 15, 1965, Walt Disney and Governor Haydon Burns announced the Florida project in Orlando, and Walt Disney World opened on October 1, 1971, with thousands of new jobs. The first rocket launched from Cape Canaveral in 1950. NASA’s Kennedy Space Center launched Apollo 11 on July 16, 1969, and astronauts walked on the moon on July 20. Houston, Texas was Mission Control, but the rockets launched from Florida. Brevard County, the Space Coast, grew from about 23,600 people in 1950 to about 230,000 in 1970, and local officials estimated that about one million people came to watch Apollo 11 launch.
Source: news reporters watching Apollo 11 lift off, July 16, 1969 (NASA)Virtue: CourageWord root: tour = to turn		 Source Study (about 30 words or more): what the photo shows and how tourism and the space program changed Florida, plus a Historian’s Sentence.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did the New Deal help Florida?Listen for: programs like the CCC and WPA paid people to build parks, roads, schools and bridges, so families had a paycheck.
  2. Why did Florida towns turn their lights off at night in 1942?Listen for: enemy submarines were near the coast, and bright lights on shore made ships easy to see.
  3. Did tourism in Florida start with Disney World?Listen for: no. Tourists came long before 1971; after World War II highways, cheaper flights and air conditioning brought even more.
  4. Did the Apollo rockets launch from Houston?Listen for: no. Houston was Mission Control; the rockets launched from the Kennedy Space Center in Florida.
  5. Who got jobs because of tourism and the space program?Listen for: lots of people, like motel clerks, cooks, tour guides, builders, teachers and orange growers, not only astronauts.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Count the jobs. Think of your last trip or outing. Who did your family pay along the way? Each one is a job.
  • One cause, many effects. Pick something new in your town, like a store or a road. What changed because of it?
  • Find the root. Conserve, service, and preserve share a part that means “keep safe.” What do tourist, detour, and return share?

See It in Florida

Optional
🚀

Many Florida state parks were built by the CCC in the 1930s, including O’Leno State Park near High Springs, where CCC workers built many of the buildings still in the park today. On the Space Coast, the Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex tells the story of the moon missions, and you can sometimes watch a real launch from the beaches nearby. Check dates and hours before you go.
