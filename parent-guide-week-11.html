This Week at a Glance

Spain wasn’t the only country that wanted Florida. In 1564, French settlers built Fort Caroline near today’s Jacksonville. In 1565, Spain founded St. Augustine and destroyed the French fort. In 1586, the English captain Francis Drake burned St. Augustine, and in 1763 England took Florida from Spain. In the second lesson your student pulls the whole unit together into causes and effects. The unit ends with the Unit 4 Assessment.

History skill
Cause and effect. Why Europeans came (God, Gold, Glory, and the race between three countries), and what happened because they did.
Virtue this unit
Courage, “both/and.” Honest historians don’t answer “good or bad.” They name the courage and the cost, with evidence for each.

Good to know: This week has the hardest history in the unit. In 1565 Spanish soldiers killed many of the French settlers at Matanzas Inlet (matanzas means “killings”). Lesson 4.04.04 explains that diseases like smallpox and measles killed tens of thousands of Florida’s first peoples within one generation, and whole nations were lost. It’s told plainly, without graphic detail. A check-in afterward can help.

Words to Know

Use them at home!
colonizationwhen one country takes control of a land and sends its own people to live there
rivalrya contest between two or more sides that want the same thing
HuguenotsFrench Protestants. Some came to Florida looking for a safe place to practice their faith.
cause / effecta cause makes something happen, and an effect is what happens because of it

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Which three countries wanted Florida? Name one place or event for each.Listen for: France and Fort Caroline, Spain and St. Augustine, England and Drake’s raid or taking Florida in 1763.
  2. What’s special about St. Augustine?Listen for: Spain founded it in 1565, and people have lived there ever since. It’s the oldest settlement like that in the U.S.
  3. What was the biggest effect on Florida’s first peoples?Listen for: disease, like smallpox and measles, which killed tens of thousands of people.
  4. Can something be brave and harmful at the same time?Listen for: “both/and,” with one example of courage and one example of harm.

What Gets Turned In

  • Each lesson: 3 evidence questions, one longer writing piece, and a Historian’s Sentence, submitted in Canvas
  • Unit 4 Assessment: pasted into Canvas when it’s finished

Done well looks like: a cause and an effect that really connect (“because…, so…”), plus a specific name, date, or place.

🏡

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Chain it. Start with “It rained, so…” and take turns adding effects. How long can the chain get?
  • Both/and. Pick a choice (staying up late, a new pet). Name one good thing and one cost.
  • How old? Find the oldest building in your town. How many years older is St. Augustine?
