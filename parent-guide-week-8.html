This Week at a Glance

Every community has to solve the same four problems: food, shelter, tools, and getting what it needs from the land. This week your student sees how Florida’s first peoples solved all four, with no stores at all. Then they study one real object, the Key Marco Cat. It’s a small wooden carving, 500 to 1,500 years old, found in the mud on Key Marco in 1896.

History skill
Seeing vs. guessing. An observation is what you can see (“I see…”). An inference is a careful guess about what it means (“Maybe…”). Historians keep the two apart.
Virtue this week
Honesty. Telling the truth about the past means saying the first peoples were skilled and clever, and admitting what one object can’t tell us.

Good to know: The lessons push back on calling Native life “simple.” If your student uses that word, ask what it would take to make a canoe from one tree or a spear point from a rock.

Words to Know

Use them at home!
artifactan object made or used by people in the past, like a pot, a tool, or a carving
observationwhat you can see for yourself. “I see a wooden figure with rounded ears.”
inferencea careful guess about what you see. “Maybe it shows a panther.”
chickeea raised platform house with a steep palm-leaf roof and open sides
💬

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did a chickee have a raised floor and no walls?Listen for: the floor kept people above floodwater and snakes, and the open sides let breezes through to keep it cool.
  2. How do you make a canoe out of one tree?Listen for: burn and scrape out the inside of a big cypress log. Some Calusa canoes were over 20 feet long.
  3. Tell me one thing you saw on the Key Marco Cat, and one thing you guessed.Listen for: an “I see…” sentence (wood, ears, kneeling) and a “Maybe…” sentence (a panther, used in a ceremony).
  4. Would you call the first peoples’ lives “simple”? Why or why not?Listen for: no, because it took a lot of skill and knowledge, passed from elders to children.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • I see / Maybe. Pull something odd from a drawer. Take turns: one “I see…” and one “Maybe…” about it.
  • Where did it come from? Name three things in your kitchen and where they came from. The first peoples got almost everything from a few miles of home.
  • Make a tool. Could you carry water with no bottle or bag? Brainstorm what you’d use from outside.
