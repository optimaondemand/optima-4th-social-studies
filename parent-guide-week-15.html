This Week at a Glance

Spain ruled Florida for about 200 years, from 1565 to 1763. This week your student traces the effects of that rule: missions across north Florida, cattle ranches, and the stone Castillo de San Marcos, which no enemy ever captured. Then comes the hardest lesson of the unit: what colonization meant for the Timucua, Apalachee, Calusa, and other Native peoples. The teacher asks the class to slow down for this one.

History skill
Cause and effect. “Because ___, ___ happened,” with evidence. Example: pirates burned St. Augustine in 1668, so Spain built a stone fort.
Virtues this week
Self-governance (Lesson 1): slowing down to ask why. Courtesy (Lesson 2): telling a hard story with care and respect for real people.

Good to know: Lesson 4.06.06 is about great loss. New diseases killed many thousands of people. Missions, forced labor, war, and slave raids broke communities apart, and most of Florida’s original tribes nearly disappeared. It’s told calmly, with no graphic detail, and it ends by noting that the Seminole and Miccosukee live in Florida today. A check-in afterward can help.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did Spain build a stone fort at St. Augustine?Listen for: English pirates burned the town in 1668, so Spain built the Castillo out of coquina to protect it.
  2. Name one effect of Spanish rule we can still see today.Listen for: the Castillo, St. Augustine itself, cattle ranching, Spanish place names, oranges.
  3. What happened to Florida’s first peoples during Spanish rule?Listen for: disease, forced labor, war and raids, and being pushed off their land. Most of the original tribes nearly disappeared.
  4. How should a historian talk about something this sad?Listen for: honestly, calmly, with evidence, and with respect. Don’t skip it and don’t make it a game.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Because chain. Pirates burned the town, so… Spain built a stone fort, so… Take turns adding a link.
  • Still here. Look up the Seminole Tribe of Florida or the Miccosukee Tribe together. What do they want people to know?
  • Shell stone. Next time you’re at the beach, look for tiny shells packed together. That’s how coquina starts.
