This Week at a Glance

This week your student meets four Native peoples who lived in Florida long before any European ship arrived: the Apalachee, Timucua, Calusa, and Tequesta. They match each people to the part of Florida where they lived. Then they compare two of them, the Timucua and the Calusa, using a drawing of a Timucua village printed in 1591.

History skill
Where you lived shaped how you lived. Your student connects the land and water in each region to what people ate, how they built, and how they got around.
Virtues this week
Service (the everyday work that kept a village fed and safe) and courtesy (using each people’s real name and getting the details right).

Good to know: The 1591 picture was made by Theodor de Bry, a European printer, from a French artist’s drawing. It is real evidence, but it was made by outsiders. The lesson asks your student to notice what the picture doesn’t show, too.

Same Florida, two very different lives. The Timucua hunted, fished rivers, and kept gardens inside fenced villages. The Calusa lived from the sea, and their leaders ruled a wide network of towns.

Words to Know

Use them at home!
regiona part of a place that is different from the parts around it, like the panhandle or the southwest coast
shell mounda hill built up from empty shells over hundreds of years. The Calusa built towns on top of them.
palisadea tall fence made of wooden poles, built around a village to protect it
primary sourcesomething made at the time by people who were there, like a drawing, a letter, or a tool

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Which of the four peoples lived closest to where we live? What was their land like?Listen for: Apalachee in the northwest panhandle, Timucua in the north and center, Calusa on the southwest coast, Tequesta on the southeast coast, and one detail about that land.
  2. Why didn’t the Calusa do much farming?Listen for: the sea gave them plenty of fish, oysters, and clams. It was a choice that fit their land, not something they couldn’t do.
  3. What does the old picture of the Timucua village show? What doesn’t it show?Listen for: the wooden fence, round houses, and fields. It can’t show what people said, believed, or felt, and it was made by Europeans.
  4. Tell me one way the Timucua and Calusa were alike, and one way they were different.Listen for: both had leaders and ceremonies and lived here a very long time; different food, homes, or travel.

🏡

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Map hunt. On a Florida map, find Tallahassee, Miami, and the Gulf coast. Which people lived near each one?
  • What’s our land good for? Step outside. If you lived here 1,000 years ago, what would you eat, build with, and travel on?
  • Same and different. Pick any two things (two pets, two stores) and name one way they’re alike and one way they’re different.
