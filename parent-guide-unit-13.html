🧭 How the Lesson Pages Work

  • 🗂️Four numbered tabs, in order. Each lesson names its own. Tab 3 is always the Self-Check, and tab 4 is where your student writes. The teacher’s texts (the Messages button) say what to do next.
  • 🧠Check is a 3-question self-check. It isn’t graded. It shows your student what to look at again before they write.
  • 💾Answers save as you type, but only in that browser on that computer. Switching computers or clearing the browser loses them.

About This Unit

Units 1 to 12 each opened one Florida File: the land and water, the first peoples, the explorers, Spanish Florida, Fort Mose and the pioneers, statehood and war, modern Florida, civil rights and citizens, and government and money. Unit 13 brings the whole year together. Your student builds a Florida History Exhibit of six slides, presents it, and writes a final reflection as a historian-citizen: someone who studies Florida’s past and takes part in Florida today.

The skill all week is checking a source: asking who made it, when, and where it is kept today, and checking what it says against other evidence. A real 1901 postcard says the stone fort in St. Augustine was begun around the 1650s; your student learned it was begun in 1672.

About the Story: The Florida Files

The whole course runs on one story. Your student’s teacher has been stuck inside Canvas all year. Each lesson she sent a message asking for help, and your student did the history and helped her along. Unit 13 is the last file, and the story ends here.

Lesson 1
The door with 13 opens onto an empty hall with a frame for every file. Your student fills the hall, and their final claim hangs on the door as the last exhibit piece. The door swings all the way open onto daylight.
Lesson 2
The teacher watches your student’s slide show from the doorway. When your student finishes their reflection, she steps out into the sunshine and thanks them by name. A closing card shows all 13 file seals.
The route
In Unit 11 your student voted on a way out, the Main Hall or the Back Stairs. The teacher’s last texts still follow that choice.

Play along: ask, “How did your teacher finally get out of Canvas?”

🔍

This Unit’s Virtue

Both lessons focus on service: using what you know to help others. Your student shows an exhibit so other people understand Florida’s story, and thinks about how they can serve their own community now.

The week has two lessons. Each lesson has four numbered tabs, so your student always knows what comes next:

Good to Know Ahead of Time

Slides are saved on one computer
The slides are saved in the browser where your student made them. If you switch computers, your student can build them again in Lesson 2, or download the PowerPoint file in Lesson 1 to keep.
The microphone
In Lesson 2, recording a voice for each slide is optional. The browser asks first. The recording and the video are made on your computer and are not uploaded anywhere unless your student turns the video in.
Your own town
One postcard in Lesson 2 is blank, for your student’s own community. Typing a town name is optional, and it stays on your computer.
A made-up picture, on purpose
In Lesson 1, one picture is shown on purpose as one that can’t be evidence, because nobody knows who made it or when. The lesson says so.
🤝

How to Help (Without Doing It for Them)

Try this

  • Be the audience for your student’s slide show, and ask one question about each slide.
  • Ask, “Who made that picture, and when?” about any picture you see together.
  • Ask the questions in the Week 31 guide. Talking first makes the reflection easier.

Try to avoid

  • Writing the slides or the reflection for them. Teachers need to see your student’s own thinking.
  • Doing the hands-on parts for them. The games are how the lesson teaches.
  • Skipping the submit step. Work isn’t turned in until it’s in Canvas.
