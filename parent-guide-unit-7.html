About This Unit

In Unit 6 Spain ruled Florida for about 200 years. In Unit 7 the flag starts changing. Your student begins with Fort Mose, a free Black town near St. Augustine, then follows Florida from Spain to Britain, back to Spain, and finally to the United States. The unit ends with the pioneer families who moved in once Florida became a U.S. territory.

It’s a short unit (two weeks, four lessons) with one big question underneath it: when a place changes hands, what happens to the people who live there?

“Who controls a place determines the rules people live under, and the people with the least power had the least say in any of it.”The big idea of Lesson 4.07.02

The Unit’s Timeline

Quick reference
  • 1693SpainThe king promises freedom to people who escape slavery in the British colonies.
  • 1738Fort MoseSpain sets up Fort Mose, a free Black town north of St. Augustine.
  • 1740Fort MoseBritish forces attack; the Fort Mose militia fights back.
  • 1763BritainSpain trades Florida to Britain. Most Spanish residents and the people of Fort Mose leave for Cuba.
  • 1783SpainAfter the American Revolution, Britain gives Florida back to Spain.
  • 1818U.S.Andrew Jackson leads U.S. troops into Spanish Florida.
  • 1819U.S.The Adams-Onís Treaty: Spain agrees to give Florida to the U.S.
  • 1821U.S.Florida becomes a U.S. territory. Pioneer families begin moving in.

This Unit’s Virtue: Courage

What it took
Walking hundreds of miles through swamps to freedom. Defending Fort Mose in 1740. Starting over in Cuba, or in a log cabin on the frontier.
Who paid
Free Black families who lost their town, and then faced harsher U.S. laws. The Seminole, whose land the pioneers settled.

Your student practices holding both: real courage, and a real cost to someone else. Some questions ask for their own opinion. Any opinion works as long as it’s backed by a detail.

How Learning Is Checked

Every lesson
Evidence questions, a Historian’s Notebook entry, and a Historian’s Sentence, submitted in Canvas. The self-check in each lesson isn’t graded.
End of unit
Unit 7 Assessment (about 45 minutes): multiple choice, a short reading on Fort Mose, one longer written answer, and a reflection on courage.

What a strong answer looks like: your student picks one group (Native peoples, free Black communities, or settlers), names a change in control, and uses evidence to explain how it changed that group’s life.

💛

Good to Know Ahead of Time

Week 17 · Hard history
Slavery in the British colonies, the dangerous escape south, and the 1740 battle at Fort Mose. Told plainly, without graphic detail.
Week 18 · Still here
The lessons point out that pioneers settled on Seminole land, and that the pressure on the Seminole led to the Seminole Wars. The Seminole Tribe of Florida is still here today.
🤝

How to Help (Without Doing It for Them)

Try this

  • Ask “Who decided?” whenever Florida changes hands.
  • Ask “Who gained, and who lost?” about the treaty.
  • Ask the questions in each weekly guide. Talking first makes the writing easier.
  • Let your student disagree with you on opinion questions, as long as they give a reason.

Try to avoid

  • Typing or wording answers for them. Teachers need to see your student’s own thinking.
  • Telling the pioneer story as if the land were empty. The lessons ask students to name whose land it was.
  • Skipping the submit step. Work isn’t turned in until it’s in Canvas.
