This Week at a Glance

This week your student goes inside a Spanish mission. A mission was a town where Spanish priests called friars taught the Catholic religion to Native people. Mission San Luis, in today’s Tallahassee, was special: the Apalachee and the Spanish shared one town and one plaza for almost 50 years (1656–1704). Then your student follows one ordinary mission workday, bell to bell, and sorts out who did which job.

History skill
Seeing both sides. The giant Apalachee Council House and the Spanish church faced each other across the same plaza. A good historian looks at both buildings.
Virtues this week
Service (Lesson 1): whom was each person serving? Perseverance (Lesson 2): who kept going through hard work, and what did it cost them?

Good to know: Both lessons are honest about the hard parts. Apalachee men were forced to work for the Spanish, sometimes carrying heavy loads on foot more than 200 miles to St. Augustine. The Spanish banned the Apalachee ball game. In 1704, with English and Creek soldiers coming, the people of San Luis burned their own town and left. None of it is graphic.

A church bell ran the day: morning prayer, field work, a shared midday meal, more work, evening prayer. Apalachee farmers grew corn, beans, and squash. Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What two buildings faced each other across the plaza at San Luis?Listen for: the Apalachee Council House and the Spanish church. The Council House was bigger.
  2. Why do historians call San Luis a “joint” settlement?Listen for: the Apalachee and the Spanish shared the town, and both had leaders living there.
  3. What foods were already here, and what did the Spanish bring?Listen for: corn, beans, and squash were already here; wheat, peaches, watermelon, cattle, pigs, and chickens came with the Spanish.
  4. What was the hardest part of mission life? For whom?Listen for: the forced work and long walks to St. Augustine, for Apalachee men and the families they left behind.

What Gets Turned In

  • Each lesson: 2–3 evidence questions, one longer writing piece, and a Historian’s Sentence, submitted in Canvas

Done well looks like: a specific detail from each side of the plaza, and a cause and effect that really connect (“The village woke early because the bell rang before sunrise”).

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Two baskets. Sort what’s in your fridge: foods from the Americas (corn, beans, squash, tomatoes) and foods brought from across the ocean.
  • Bell to bell. What “bells” run your family’s day (an alarm, a school start time)? Who decided them?
  • Biggest room. The Council House held over a thousand people. What’s the biggest room you’ve been in?
