  • 📋Everything is gathered at the end. The bottom of the Assignment tab collects every answer from the lesson in one place.

  • 🔊Listen at the top reads the page aloud, with a speed control. Keep a paper Historian’s Notebook and a pencil nearby.

About This Unit

In Unit 3 your student met the peoples who lived in Florida for thousands of years. In this unit, ships arrive. Your student follows the first Spanish explorers, learns why they came, watches Spain, France, and England fight over the same coast, and then weighs what all of it meant for the people who already lived here.

It’s a short unit with big ideas: putting events in order, naming motives, and explaining cause and effect. Through all of it, your student is asked to tell the story honestly, including the courage and the cost.

“Their arrival was not a discovery; it was a meeting between strangers and the nations who had been living on this land for thousands of years.”The big idea of Unit 4, from Lesson 4.04.01

About the Story: The Florida Files

The whole course runs on one story. Your student’s teacher is stuck inside Canvas. She can find old Florida files in there, but she can’t open them, so each lesson she sends a message asking for help. Your student does the history work, and the file unlocks. There are 13 files, one for each unit. Unit 4 is File 4.

This Unit’s Virtue: Courage, Through a Lens

What it took
Crossing an ocean nobody had mapped, in a wooden ship. French settlers starting over in a strange land. Native nations holding on to who they were while three powers fought around them.
What it cost
Disease, lost villages, and land taken by force. The explorers’ courage was used for something, and the lessons ask whether that was a wise use.

Your student practices answering “both/and” instead of “good or bad,” with evidence for each side. Some questions ask for their honest opinion. Any opinion works as long as it’s backed by a detail.

How a Week Works

Every week has two lessons. Each one follows the same four steps, so your student always knows what comes next:

📍 LearnThe teacher’s message, the big question, and the virtue
🔍 ExploreRead the story and study a source
🧠 Check3 quick questions, not graded
📝 AssignmentAnswer, write, and turn in
  • 📅Timeline Thread: dates in order, so the story builds
  • 📜A real source: a royal order, or a page from a mission baptism book
  • ❓3 evidence questions answered with details from the lesson
  • 📓One longer piece: a notebook entry or a source study response
  • ✏️Historian’s Sentence: “I learned that ___ because ___.”
  • 📬Turn-in checklist at the bottom, ending with submitting in Canvas

How Learning Is Checked

Every lesson
Evidence questions, one longer writing piece, and a Historian’s Sentence, submitted in Canvas. The self-check in each lesson isn’t graded.
End of unit
Unit 4 Assessment (about 45 minutes): multiple choice, a short task on a Spanish royal decree, two longer written answers, and a reflection on courage. Notes are allowed.

What “secure” looks like on the assessment: your student puts events in the right order, connects a cause to an effect, and imagines how a Native person in the 1500s might have seen these events, using evidence.

💛

Good to Know Ahead of Time

Week 11 · Hard history
The 1565 killings of French settlers at Matanzas Inlet, and the diseases that killed tens of thousands of Florida’s first peoples. Both are told plainly, without graphic detail.
All unit · Word choice
The lessons don’t call this a “discovery,” because people had already lived here for thousands of years. Also, the Fountain of Youth is a legend that was added later.

How to Help (Without Doing It for Them)

Try this

  • Ask “Who was already living there?” whenever an explorer comes up.
  • Ask “What happened because of that?” to practice cause and effect.
  • Ask the questions in each weekly guide. Talking first makes the writing easier.
  • Let your student disagree with you on the opinion questions, as long as they give a reason.

Try to avoid

  • Typing or wording answers for them. Teachers need to see your student’s own thinking.
  • Settling the courage questions for them. Weighing both sides is the skill.
  • Skipping the submit step. Work isn’t turned in until it’s in Canvas.
